# Figure 5 — legend

**Fig. 5 | Biomass estimates from lipid fingerprints recover ClimGrass community composition and a drought-associated bacterial decline.**

**a**, Estimated biomass composition of 12 ClimGrass soils (positive ionisation; 736 spectrally verified features, 16 phyla). Bars are fc-weighted estimates converted from matched lipid signal to biomass using group-specific total lipid content (Bacteria 7%, Fungi 10%, Viridiplantae 2.5%, Animalia 18%, Protists 10%, Archaea 6% dry mass; Table S6) with 95% reference-sample bootstrap confidence intervals. Grey segments are literature biomass ranges (Table S6). Bacteria (38.4%), Viridiplantae (27.0%), Fungi (19.6%) and Archaea (6.5%) fall within those ranges; Animalia (5.7%) lies just above the 1–5% expectation and Protists (2.9%) above the <1% expectation.

**b**, Drought (x) and warming (y) log₂ fold changes for the 16 analysis phyla (*n* = 12 soils). Point size is mean attributed abundance; heavier outline marks unadjusted *p* < 0.05. No phylum reaches FDR *q* < 0.05. Pseudomonadota declines under drought (log₂ −0.60, *p* = 0.005, FDR *q* = 0.08); Evosea is nominally drought-depleted (nominal *p* = 0.050). Other bacterial (Actinomycetota, Bacillota) and fungal (Ascomycota, Basidiomycota, Mucoromycota) phyla are labelled for reference but are not significant. Warming effects are not significant.
